# **The Goddess in a Statue And Why Good Girls Don't Get Angry**

Somebody said it to you before you were ten.

Maybe it was your mother. Maybe an aunt, a teacher, a neighbour who had authority inside your house for reasons nobody ever explained to you. The wording changes by family. The instruction does not. Don't shout. Don't make that face. Don't be difficult. Nobody likes an angry girl. Good girls don't get angry.

You did not argue. You were six. At six you do not evaluate instructions, you absorb them, and the ones delivered with the most feeling go in the deepest. It was probably said to you at a moment when you were correct. That is the part people forget. Most girls are not taught to swallow anger during a tantrum. They are taught it during a legitimate protest. Something was taken, something was unfair, someone lied about what happened, and the volume of your objection became the entire subject of the conversation. The thing you were objecting to was never discussed again.

That is the first lesson. Not that anger is wrong. That anger is louder than the truth it is carrying, and will be used to bury it.

The woman who taught you this was taught it too. She was six once, in a smaller house, with less room. She was not being cruel. She was passing on the only survival protocol she had been given, and she was passing it on because she loved you and because she had watched what happens to women who do not learn it. This is not a conspiracy. It is a relay. Every generation hands the baton forward and nobody stops to ask what is in it.

What is in it is a forgery.

Because here is what the instruction claims to be. It claims to be discipline. Restraint. Maturity. The mastery of a lower impulse by a higher self. It arrives wrapped in the vocabulary of spiritual development, and in this country it arrives with scripture attached, which makes it almost impossible to refuse. Anger is one of the six enemies. Krodha. Listed alongside greed and lust and delusion. You were not simply asked to be quiet. You were told that your quiet was holy.

And now look at the statue.

She is standing on the chest of her own husband. Her tongue is out. She is wearing a garland of severed heads and a skirt of severed arms and her eyes are red and she is black as the space between stars. She is not a metaphor for something gentler. Nobody rendered her that way by accident. Every temple, every calendar, every sticker on the back of every truck, and in a very large number of the houses where a girl is currently being told to lower her voice, she is standing in the puja room.

We worship her. We do her aarti. We name our daughters after her.

And then we tell those daughters that a woman who raises her voice has failed at being a woman.

Both things are true in the same house on the same day and nobody experiences it as a contradiction. That is how you know the instruction was never about spiritual development. If anger were genuinely the enemy, we would not have built our most powerful goddess out of it. We would not have put her at the centre of the room. What actually happened is more ordinary than a philosophical error. Somebody worked out a long time ago that a woman with access to her own fire is expensive to manage, and somebody found a vocabulary that made the removal of that fire sound like her own idea.

The cut was made. It was sold as fire safety. It was a confiscation.

---

Nine years of clinical practice have taught me to ask questions that have nothing to do with food.

One of them is when you were last angry.

The women almost never remember. Not a vague answer, a blank one. They search for it the way you search for a word in a language you stopped speaking. And then, very often, they tell me they are not an angry person. They tell me this the way you would report a qualification. There is pride in it. I have never once raised my voice at my children. I don't fight with anyone. I just adjust.

I want you to sit with what that sentence actually means. Not what it sounds like. What it means. It means that across four or five decades, in a life that contained at minimum the standard allotment of betrayal and unfairness and people taking what was not theirs, this nervous system never once completed the sequence it was built to complete. Something arrived, something registered, and then nothing came out. Every time. For forty years.

The energy did not evaporate. Physiology does not work that way. There is no exit in this system marked *unfelt*. It went somewhere and it became something, and by the time she reaches me it usually has a name and a diagnostic code attached to it.

There is a question underneath the panel and the protocol, and eventually I ask it. What did they take from you, and how old were you when they took it?

---

So let me say the thing plainly, and let me not soften it on the way out.

Anger is sacred.

Not acceptable. Not valid. Not a difficult emotion we must learn to hold with compassion. Sacred. It is the force that clears. It is the only energy in the human system that is designed to remove an obstacle, and a life contains obstacles, and some of them are load-bearing, and they do not come down because you understood them or forgave them or breathed through them. Some things come down because force was applied.

She is not standing in that room because we are a violent people. She is standing there because our ancestors understood something that we have since decided to be embarrassed about. When the demons could not be defeated by the gods, when the strategy failed and the negotiation failed and the assembled masculine force of the entire cosmos was insufficient, what got called was her. The destroying feminine. The one who does not discuss it. Her fury is not a lapse in her divinity. It is her divinity.

That force is in you. It is not a metaphor and it is not borrowed. Every woman reading this has it and most of you have been trained to experience its arrival as a personal failure.

Here is what anger is actually doing when it comes. It is telling you where the cage is.

Look at what makes you furious and you will find the wall you have been pressed against. Sometimes it was built by other people. Family, institution, a marriage, a workplace, a religion, a country. Sometimes you built it yourself, years ago, as a shelter. That is important. A lot of what you will need to destroy was originally constructed to protect you. It worked. It kept you alive in a season that required it. And it has now outlived that season and it is still standing and it has become the exact thing preventing your next twenty years.

Anger is what you feel when a part of you has already outgrown a structure that the rest of you is still living inside.

It is not a malfunction. It is intelligence arriving with sufficient force to be heard over your conditioning. That is why it is loud. It has to be. It is competing with everything you were taught before you were ten.

---

Now the discipline. Because there is one, and this is where I will not flatter you.

Fire clears the path. Fire also takes the forest. It is the same fire. Nothing about the fire changes between those two outcomes. What changes is where it was pointed and whether anyone was holding the line.

Dynamite drives a tunnel through a mountain. Dynamite also flattens the mountain. Same charge. The engineer and the vandal are working with identical material. The difference is that one of them knows precisely what he wants removed.

This is the real cut, and it is not the one you were sold. You were told the question was whether to have the fire. The question was always aim.

Most anger that causes damage is not too much anger. It is correctly sized anger discharged at the wrong target because the right target was too dangerous, too far away, or too beloved. The woman who cannot say the true sentence to her mother says a smaller cruel one to her daughter. The man who cannot confront his employer confronts his wife. The fire was real, the reading was accurate, the aim was cowardly. And then everyone concludes that the fire was the problem, and the actual obstacle stands there for another decade, untouched.

So when it arrives, before anything else: what is this pointing at.

Not who is in front of you. What is it pointing at. Those are different questions with different answers and the gap between them is where most people spend their lives.

And then the second discipline, which nobody tells you because it is unglamorous.

Do not live there.

Anger is a sympathetic state. Full fight physiology. Cortisol, adrenaline, glucose dumped into the blood for a fight that in modern life almost never physically happens. It burns magnesium. It burns B vitamins. It eats your sleep. It shuts down digestion and repair, because a body that thinks it is under attack does not build tissue, it spends. Sustained, it produces exactly the burnout picture I see in women who finally found their anger at forty-two and then lived inside it for three years because it felt so much better than the numbness.

It does feel better than the numbness. That is not an illusion. It is also not somewhere you can stay.

The instrument you want for ordinary life is the ventral vagal state. Rest, connection, digestion, repair, the capacity to solve a problem without detonating anything. That is the default. That is where the majority of a good life is lived and where the majority of problems are actually solved.

But some problems are not going to be solved from there. Some walls do not come down through conversation. When anger is needed, anger is needed, and a woman who has trained herself out of access to it will stand in front of that wall for thirty years describing it beautifully to her friends.

Fire in the hearth. Not the house on fire, and not a cold house either.

---

There is one more layer and I have put it here deliberately, at the end, because if I had put it at the beginning you would have used it to escape.

Anger is very often standing in front of grief.

Underneath the rage there is frequently something that was never mourned. A loss nobody acknowledged. A childhood that was not what it should have been. A marriage that was over years before it ended. A thing that was done to you that the people around you agreed not to discuss. Grief needs a witness. It cannot complete without one. And if the world offered you no witness, the grief could not be finished, and something had to stand guard over an open wound in a place that was not safe.

Anger took that post. It is armour and it is doing a job.

Now watch how this gets used against you. The moment a woman becomes forceful, someone will tell her she is not really angry, she is hurt. Said gently. Said with what appears to be enormous compassion. And it works, because it is partly true, and she softens, and she cries, and everyone in the room relaxes, and the obstacle she was about to remove is still standing exactly where it was.

That is the same confiscation as the one performed on you at six. It has simply changed costume. Shame was the crude version. This is the refined one, and it is much harder to refuse, because it comes dressed as care.

So let me be precise. The armour is not a lie about the wound. It was a correct response to a world that had no room for the wound. You do not take a person's armour off while they are still standing in the field.

You take it off after. When the fire has done its work and the path is clear and the obstacle is gone and there is finally somewhere safe to put it down. Then the grief comes, and it is enormous, and it is the actual thing, and it can finally be finished because there is now room for it that did not exist before.

The fire does not replace the grief. It clears the ground the grief needs in order to be felt.

Which means the sequence most of you were given was backwards. You were told to feel your sadness and let go of your anger. In practice, for most women I have worked with, the anger has to come first. It removes what is in the way. And then, in the space it made, the thing you have been carrying since you were nine finally gets to be put down.

---

I have never been a quiet woman.

My anger built most of what I have. It ended the arrangements that needed ending, it walked me out of rooms I had no business staying in, and it is the reason I stopped accepting an explanation for my own body that did not hold up. People have found the intensity of it difficult. I have been told, at various points, in various registers, that it is my central flaw. I understand why they say it. I have learned to aim it, which took me most of my life and which I would not describe as finished.

I am not ashamed of it. Not slightly. Not for the sake of appearing gracious in front of anyone.

There is a version of a woman that people find comfortable. Pleasant, smiling, softly spoken, endlessly available, never quite taking up the whole chair. I know that woman. I have been asked to be her many times.

Nobody carved a statue of her.

Go and look at the one they did carve. Her tongue is out. A garland of heads. Her foot on the chest of a god. She has been standing there the entire time you were being taught to lower your voice, watching, waiting for somebody in that house to make the connection.

That is you. That was always you.

The only question is what you point it at.
