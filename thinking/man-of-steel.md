# **The Composed Man**

### **Why the calmest man in the room cannot actually guard it**

You know this guy.. he is the one everybody brings their problems to.

Capable and composed, basically unflappable even during the biggest crisis. Everyone calls him the calm in the storm. Nothing in his manner suggests anything is wrong, and nothing in his life suggests it either. He earns. He shows up. He does not fall apart.

Ask him what he is feeling and you will get one of three answers.

An "err.. I don't know " or"nothing " or a thought wearing a feeling's clothing, which is the one I find most impressive, because it is so convincing. "I feel like this was mishandled." That is not a feeling. That is a verdict.

He is not lying to you. He genuinely does not know.

And, before I go further I do want to state that some women get here too, because I do not want a composed woman reading this to decide she is the exception.

The difference is not who freezes. It is what happens next.

A woman who goes numb is called cold, a complaint against her. A man who goes numb is called strong, his status rises and I think that is the whole reason this phenomenon concentrates in men, and it is also why he never examines it. You do not investigate the thing everyone keeps thanking you, praisinging you for.

**Two notebooks**

To understand what happened to these men you have to understand that their brain kept (and it continues to keep) two separate records of their life, and they did not talk to each other.

The first record is for stories. For eg. We went to the beach, I lost a shoe, my mother laughed. That notebook needs language and a sense of self and a hippocampus that has finished a certain amount of wiring, so it does not come online properly until somewhere around three or four.

The second is for habits. That one is open from the day he was born or possibly earlier. It does not write words and it does not need a spoken language. It writes how tight his shoulders sit at rest. How fast his heart goes when a voice changes pitch. Whether he expects the person walking towards him to be kind. Whether his gut releases or clamps when someone stands too close.

So when something hard happened to him at two, nothing went into the story notebook. There is no story. Nothing to recall, nothing to describe, nothing to bring to a therapist and lay on the table because the brain did not have the hardware to record this software.

But the habit notebook took it down in full. And that notebook is always open. It never stopped writing. Which means he is carrying a complete, detailed, perfectly preserved record of something he cannot access, in a format that does not feel like memory at all.

It feels like personality.

That is the entire trick. It does not present as a wound. It presents as this is just how I am.

**The two notebooks have addresses**

This is not a metaphor I invented for comfort. The two records run on different hardware, and the hardware matures on different schedules.

The story notebook depends on the hippocampus, working with the prefrontal cortex. The hippocampus is what binds a moment into an episode you can later retrieve as an event, with a where and a when attached. It is not finished at birth. It carries on wiring through the first few years, and the capacity for durable autobiographical memory does not consolidate until somewhere around three or four. This is why almost nobody remembers being two. The events happened. The recording equipment for that particular format had not been installed.

The habit notebook runs on structures that are working much earlier. The amygdala, which learns threat associations. The striatum and cerebellum, which hold procedure and sequence. The brainstem nuclei that set an autonomic tone. And the interoceptive maps, which record the state of the body itself.

None of these produce a narrative. They produce a setting.

So a threat association formed at eighteen months does exactly what it was built to do. It persists. It shapes heart rate and muscle tone and the reading of a face. And it comes with no retrievable episode attached, because the equipment that would have attached one was not yet running.

The man is not repressing anything. There is nothing to repress. There was never a story in the first place.

**Nobody is born knowing how to feel**

Here is the part the wellness industry has never absorbed, because it complicates the merchandise.

Feeling is a taught skill. Not an inherited one.

The body produces signals. A tight stomach. A hot face. A fluttering in the chest. Every infant has all of it, in full, from the beginning. What no infant has is the map from the signal to the name. The vocabulary itself.

Someone has to sit with him and do the naming. Over and over.

"This feeling- that's sadness, isn't it." "That's nervous." "You're angry, and it is alright to be angry."

A thousand small acts of translation, delivered by an adult regulated enough to be looking at him properly.

That is how a human learns to feel.

Now remove the teacher. The child still generates every signal on schedule. What never gets built is the dictionary. The signal comes in, there is no word waiting for it, and the whole thing lands as static.

Twenty years later you ask him what he is feeling and he says he doesn't know, and he is being completely honest with you.

**What the naming actually builds**

The signal has a route.

Information about the internal state of the body, from the gut, the heart, the viscera, travels by the vagus and by spinal pathways into the brainstem, up through the thalamus, and arrives in the posterior insula. That is the raw map. Pressure, temperature, tension, rate. Data with no meaning attached.

The map becomes a feeling further forward, in the anterior insula, working alongside the anterior cingulate. This is where a body state becomes a felt state with a name on it and a reason to act.

That forward integration is built by experience. It is not delivered fully formed. A caregiver naming the state for a child, over and over, is the input that constructs it. And there is a finding here that people usually get backwards: putting a feeling into words is not merely describing it. Affect labelling engages the prefrontal cortex and quiets the amygdala. Naming is part of the regulation, not a report filed afterwards.

So the child who was never named for does not simply lack vocabulary. He is missing a piece of the apparatus that turns a body state into a usable signal.

Medicine has a term for the result. Alexithymia. Difficulty identifying feelings, difficulty describing them, and a way of thinking that stays pointed at external facts rather than internal states. It is measurable. It is consistently found at higher rates in men. And it travels with exactly the clinical picture I am describing: functional gut disorders, hypertension, chronic somatic symptoms that never resolve on any protocol.

He is not somatising because he is dramatic. He is somatising because the signal has nowhere else to go.

**For him it was not an absence. It was a confiscation.**

This is where boys and girls part.

A girl usually gets some naming. Imperfect, inconsistent, often wrong, but some. The dictionary gets built badly rather than not at all.

A boy gets the naming reversed.

The signal arrives and the instruction is not that “this is sadness”. The instruction is to stop that. Not a gap in the teaching. Training in the opposite direction, delivered by everyone he trusts, for eighteen years, with love. His father, who was doing what was done to him. His mother, who was frightened of what the world does to soft boys and was not wrong. His teachers. His friends, who learned it a year before he did and enforced it on him in the way children enforce things, which is to say without mercy.

Every one of them believed they were preparing him.

And then, and this is the part nobody says, the adaptation gets rewarded.

He goes quiet under pressure and he is told he is mature. He does not react and he is told he is steady. He absorbs something that should have levelled him and does not mention it and he is described, admiringly, as unshakeable. He is promoted for it. He is chosen for it. Women marry him for exactly this quality and then spend fifteen years trying to find him.

So the shell is not a symptom he is hiding. It is the achievement he is being congratulated for.

Which is precisely why it never gets examined, and why he arrives in front of me at forty five with a gut that has not worked properly in eleven years, wanting to know about his thyroid.

**What he looks like by then**

He does not come to me for this. He comes for the digestion, or the sleep, or the fatigue that no panel explains, or the blood pressure that will not come down on three medications.

Then somewhere in the consultation I ask what something was like for him. And there is a pause. And then: I don't know. Tired, maybe. It was fine.

Good news does not fully land. Bad news does not either. He watches other people cry at things and runs a quiet audit on himself about what is missing.

Some of them describe watching their own life from slightly outside it. Through glass is the phrase I hear most.

A great many of them are drawn to intensity, and for men it takes a specific and respectable shape. The training that goes past training. The risk taken for no particular return. The argument he did not need to have. The crisis he is unusually available for, and unusually calm inside, and slightly reluctant to see end. This is not a taste for challenge. Intensity is the only volume at which he can still hear himself, and he has organised a large part of his life around finding it.

And he very often cannot tell you what he wants.

This one surprises people but there's a reason for it. Wanting is a feeling. Preference runs on signal. Take out the signal and you get a man who is genuinely excellent at everything except knowing what he would like for dinner, which sounds like a joke until you watch what it does to a relationship.

Because a man who cannot locate a preference cannot hold a boundary. A boundary is just a preference, enforced. So he presents as easy going, accommodating, happy to go along with it, and then detonates six months later over something small, and everyone including him treats the detonation as the problem.

**The stillness is not rest**

Unfortunately this gets read backwards most often, including by clinicians, so I am stating it explicitly.

Their calm is not real calm.

Underneath it the threat system is running at a raised baseline and has been running that way for his entire life. It never received the message that the danger period ended. It could not have. That message would have to arrive in the habit notebook, and nothing new has been written there since.

So the body sits braced. Jaw. Shoulders. Gut. Sleep that is technically happening and never gets deep. Digestion that is unreliable in ways no elimination diet has ever fixed, because the enteric nervous system sits downstream of a nervous system that has not once been told it is safe.

He reads faces for mood shifts before anyone speaks, and he is almost always right, because he has been reading rooms since before he could talk. Survival skill first. Professional asset later. Exhausting throughout.

Small things set off reactions far too large for the trigger, and then get clamped down inside a second. What you see is a flicker, and then flatness again.

Living inside a clamped system literally has been this person's whole life.

In his case, numbness and high alert are not opposites. They are one system. The threat response is always running. The numbing is the lid that got built on top to make it liveable enough to get through school.

Which is why a man can be genuinely shut down and genuinely wired at the same time. And why the flatness sometimes breaks into rage that appears to arrive from nowhere.

It did not arrive from nowhere. It was always running. The lid slipped.

And there is a consequence that gets mistaken for a character flaw. In this state the thinking part of the brain is only partly available. Not because it is damaged. Because when the system reads unsafe, resources go to scanning and readiness rather than reflection. Men call themselves stupid for this. They are not stupid. They are allocated elsewhere.

**Now I am going to stop talking about him**

Because you have been reading this at a comfortable distance, and the distance was deliberate, and it ends here.

I have written before about the two roles. The masculine as guardian. Protector of the realm. Not the owner of it. The vault door does not own the diamond.

Here is what I did not have language for then.

Guarding runs on feeling. There is no separate faculty.

The channel that tells you your own chest has gone tight is the identical channel that tells you the man across the table is lying. There is no second system. Interoception and threat detection are one instrument, and it reads inward and outward at once, and you cannot switch off the inward half and keep the outward half working.

And I mean that structurally, not poetically.

The anterior insula is active when you sense your own internal state. It is also active when you infer someone else's. The better a person reads their own interior, the better they read other people's, and this relationship shows up consistently enough that it is no longer in serious dispute.

There is no second organ for reading the room. The instrument that tells you your own chest has gone tight is the same instrument you use to detect that something is wrong with your daughter before she says anything.

Which means the sentence I have been building towards is not a metaphor either.

Shut down the inward channel and you have degraded the outward one. Not as a consequence. As the same event.

So when you were four and someone told you to stop that, and you did, and everyone was pleased with you, what got shut down was not softness.

It was the radar.

And a guardian without a radar has exactly two options, and you have watched both of them your whole life.

The first is that he does not notice. Something is wrong in his own house and it has been wrong for years and he is the last to know, because the signal that would have told him is precisely the signal he was trained out of receiving. He finds out when it is already catastrophic. Then he says nobody told him, and he is right, and he is also the one who was supposed to be able to tell without being told.

The second is that everything reads as a threat. The radar is not dead but flooded, no gradation, no discrimination between a real breach and a bad mood. This is the man whose family manages him. Who checks. Who needs to know where. Who calls it protection and cannot understand why nobody in the house seems protected.

And this is the mechanism I have been circling for years without being able to name it.

Possession is what guarding degrades into when the felt sense fails.

If you can feel the space, you do not have to own it. You know when something is off, you move, and then you go back to rest. That is the design. It is economical. It requires almost nothing most of the time.

If you cannot feel the space, the only way to be certain it is safe is to fix everything in it in place and keep it there. Control every variable, because you have lost the instrument that would have told you which variable actually mattered.

That is not evil. Not at the start. It is a man doing the job with a broken instrument and reaching for the only substitute available.

It becomes evil later. It becomes the guard who owns the treasure. But it does not begin there, and if we keep telling men that the inversion is a moral failure we will keep getting nowhere, because you cannot moralise a man back into a capacity that was taken out of him before he could speak.

He does not need to be told to be better. He needs the instrument back.

**Why understanding this changes nothing**

You can know all of it.

You can name every person, sequence every event, produce dates. You can have an articulate, accurate, genuinely sophisticated account of exactly what was done to you and by whom.

And feel nothing about it.

I have sat across from men who have spent eleven years in talk therapy and can explain their own childhood better than I can explain mine, and who are still braced, still flat, still sleeping four hours.

They think this means they are broken but that's further from the truth. The thing is knowing is written in the story notebook. The pattern lives in the habit notebook. The two notebooks cannot read each other because the wiring is missing.

This is not a failure of effort and it is not a failure of the therapist either, and I want to say that plainly, because the correction I am making is not the fashionable one. Insight is not useless. It is simply written in the wrong language for the notebook that is actually running your physiology.

The habit notebook updates under two conditions, and it needs both.

The old feeling has to actually show up. Live. In the body. Present tense. Not described from a safe distance in the past tense with excellent vocabulary.

And something new has to happen at the same time. Something the pattern does not expect and cannot account for.

That mismatch is the entire mechanism that is going to rewrite how this person experiences life in the future.

Which is why this work goes through the body. Not because the body is fashionable at the moment, and not because thinking is the enemy. Because you are not arguing with a belief. You are rewriting a notebook that does not read words.

**The layer below that**

I will name what I actually think is true and you can take it or leave it.

Sri Aurobindo called this region the subconscient. Not the unconscious of Western psychology, which is largely repressed material that was once conscious and could in principle be recalled. The subconscient is older than that. It is the layer where the body and the earliest life store their habits, below memory, below thought, running the machine from underneath.

He was explicit that it does not answer to reasoning. You cannot argue with it. You cannot instruct it. It gives ground slowly and it gives ground under the pressure of a Force that is not the mind's.

I did not need the framework to see this clinically. I saw it in panels and in client work for years before I had language for it. But the framework explains why the most intelligent men I work with are so often the most stuck. They have brought a magnificent instrument to a job that instrument cannot do, and then concluded, reasonably, that the job must be impossible.

It also explains why so much of the emotional release work on offer does not reach this. Feel your feelings. Let the anger be heard. Express instead of suppress. All of that is vital level work, and it is genuinely useful, and it does not touch the subconscient. Which is why the man who has been diligently feeling his feelings for a decade is still braced.

**What I want you to take**

Three things.

Nothing is wrong with you. What you are carrying is not a defect of character. It is an accurate adaptation, built early, by a system doing its job well under conditions that warranted it, and then reinforced by every institution you passed through.

You are not going to think your way out. The years you spent trying were not wasted. They were aimed at the wrong notebook.

And the absence of memory never meant the absence of injury. The reassurance you were given, that you were too young for it to matter, was exactly backwards. You were young enough that it went into the record that never closes.

Which is the good news, and there is some.

The notebook is still open. It is still taking dictation. Today, while you read this.

Which means it is still available to be written in.

And the man who gets his radar back is not diminished by it. He is not softened, or made less, or converted into something other than what he is.

He is restored to the thing he was built to be, and could never do, because the instrument he needed was taken from him before he had words to object.
